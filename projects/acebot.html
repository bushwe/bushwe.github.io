Acebott QD001 Smart Robot Car + QD007 Robotic Arm

Designed, assembled, calibrated, and programmed a mobile robotic platform: an ESP32-based smart car integrated with a multi-axis, servo-driven robotic arm. The full build covered mechanical assembly, electrical wiring, Bluetooth communication, embedded firmware, and — the biggest part — systematic troubleshooting to get the drive system and arm running together.

Engineering Work

  • Assembled the complete kit vehicle — drivetrain, chassis, and mecanum wheels — interpreting technical assembly diagrams and correcting fit issues along the way
  • Assembled a multi-degree-of-freedom robotic arm with MG90S servo-driven joints and a gear-driven gripper
  • Wired drive motors, servos, and power distribution; routed cabling clear of moving parts
  • Calibrated each servo to a defined zero position and verified joint motion ranges before final assembly
  • Configured Bluetooth control through the Acebott app and uploaded Arduino-compatible firmware

Troubleshooting & Outcome

  • Arm initially unresponsive; later, enabling the arm broke driving — traced to firmware operating modes
  • Isolated faults by testing each servo individually instead of swapping parts at random
  • Repositioned servo horns after finding incorrect initial alignment
  • When the arm and gripper claw physically broke — a snapped servo horn and gear linkage — I engineered a repair instead of replacing parts: rebuilt the joints with contact cement and added structural supports to reinforce the weak points
  • Result: fully functional robot car + arm, remotely operated from a mobile app

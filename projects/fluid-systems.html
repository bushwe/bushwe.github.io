Fluid Systems: Pump, Multiline Flow & Hydraulic Lift

A three-stage fluid mechanics design project: build a positive displacement pump to move water between reservoirs, split flow across a multiline pipe network, then convert the system into a working hydraulic lift. I handled calculations and data recording, and contributed to the design — which we iterated from sketch to working system on the spot when the materials demanded it.

Engineering Work

  • Designed a syringe-driven pump using a Y-connector and dual check-style valve arrangement
  • Measured mass flow rates across trials (~0.024–0.033 kg/s) and computed volume flow, averages, and standard deviation
  • Compared flow behavior in small vs. large diameter lines — observed the velocity/pressure trade-off firsthand
  • Built a hydraulic lift driving two 20 mL syringes from one 60 mL master syringe

Problems Solved

  • Scrapped the original design when materials arrived and rebuilt a simpler, more modular layout
  • Traced volume losses in the hydraulic system to leakage and trapped air; proposed shorter tube runs and an air-bleed valve
  • Identified that consistent, controlled pump pressure — not maximum speed — eliminated most leakage
